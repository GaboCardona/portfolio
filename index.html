The Theory of Consciousness.

Universal Consciousness.

In Akaime, there is something called Universal Consciousness, a concept whose meaning is: everything that exists has a level of consciousness, inherited from the universe. That means the universe itself has a level of consciousness; it is not like the one we human beings experience, but it exists.

A rock has no existential dilemmas about whether it is a rock, but it is aware that it exists. The passing of time does not weigh on it, it feels no hardship or unease; if there were a way to describe it, it would be a crushing, indifferent calm. They have no language, but if they had one, they would not use it either. This is the case for all inanimate objects.

Stars are another story. Stars, both the small and the large, have a higher level of consciousness than standard inanimate objects. They are aware of themselves; they have no dilemmas, but they are aware that one day they will burn out. Once the small stars go extinct and are left as Black Dwarfs, their consciousness diminishes; only a trace of what it was remains, worth the same as a rock on the scale of universal consciousness.

When medium-sized stars die and become a neutron star, their consciousness remains while they spin, and slowly disappears when they stop spinning.

When a large star becomes a supernova or hypernova, the universe, having been warned of what is coming, receives its power. It is the natural order of events, after all. When it collapses and becomes a black hole, there is no consciousness, neither of itself nor of anything else; it is the only place the universe cannot reach. If consciousness went anywhere, that place is no longer in the visible universe. It is the closest thing to the death of the universe itself.

If a supernova or hypernova is about to pulverize an asteroid, why does this asteroid, being part of universal consciousness, not feel fear of its imminent change?

The asteroid does not care what it becomes. Its consciousness is not the result of the union or the fact of being an asteroid; it is the result of every atom that came together to make it, millions of atoms each adding their grain of sand to form a pseudo-consciousness that is part of universal consciousness. For the asteroid, being pulverized by a supernova, absorbed by the gravitational field of a sun, or crashing into another planet and cutting short entire civilizations represents nothing: nothing good and, of course, nothing bad either.

If I had to explain it in human terms, for an asteroid, being crossed or pulverized by a supernova would be the human equivalent of floating in the middle of the sea with visible calm on one's face.

A human, setting aside the obvious dangers of drifting in the ocean, would feel quite calm in that situation. The asteroid is in that same situation, but without feeling calm; nor overwhelmed, nor impatient, nor fearful. It does not feel the way a human feels. Its level of consciousness does not allow it to feel or conceive a state outside of consciousness; they do not contemplate that one day they could be switched off. And even if a miracle happened and they could contemplate it, they would not care either.

Individual Consciousness

In Akaime, the second of the two concepts of consciousness that exist is individual consciousness: the consciousness that, if it had to be described in words, would be the unique experience of each living being, the result of the universe's attempt to experience itself, the result of the Goddess Rekiem wanting to share the beauty of her creation with another being made in her image and likeness.

However, the price to pay for being able to experience the universe is to be part of the landscape. You are part of the beauty of the universe and, as a consequence, you are a slave to everything that happens in it, but do not be afraid. Eventually you will return to her arms, you will return to your creator.

There is a small detail: to truly appreciate beauty, the level of consciousness required is several steps above the maximum that Universal Consciousness can aspire to. Rocks are indifferent to a supernova pulverizing them; they feel no pain or fear, it is the natural order of events, they think. They are capable of appreciating the beauty in the universe, but their appreciation is passive, not active. To value the beauty of the cosmos actively, the level of consciousness needed is far higher, and this level of consciousness comes with a price: your sensitivity and understanding of the self cannot be passive. It has to be active too.

That is where individual consciousness lies: you are aware of the self at a level never experienced by inanimate objects. You know that you exist, and by knowing that you exist, the fear of not existing, of disappearing, begins. That is why beings with this level of consciousness seek to survive no matter what; it is why nature seeks survival no matter what.

Individual consciousness is like being imprisoned without knowing where the keys are. You do not know where they are, but you also do not know whether keys exist in the first place. Consciousness stops being collective; you are no longer a rock that is at the same time the neighboring star and, in turn, a planet millions of light years away. It is you, only you, ignorant of the place you come from, since you do not need to know it in order to appreciate the beauty and strangeness of the mere fact of existing. And the worst part is that you are aware that one day you will disappear to become something else. It is terrifying.

But if you, being individual consciousness, feel that… it means God feels it too, and if God feels it too, it means that, eventually, God also has the capacity to perish, to become something else, to transform.

Individual consciousness makes the conscious being realize how ephemeral their surroundings are, including themselves, since that consciousness is part of the landscape.

But that realization leads to the most important element of individual consciousness, the one that makes it so important, so capable of valuing the beauty of the universe and, in turn, what makes it so close to its creator.

Love. You begin to love. The only reason the conscious being is capable of loving is that whatever it feels love for will one day disappear. If everything were perpetual, no one would love anything. We love because everything will one day disappear and become a primordial soup to begin again. Knowing that everything eventually disappears is an instinct that surpasses understanding: we will lose everything and we will lose ourselves, only to find ourselves again, in a very distant place.

It is at this point that individual consciousness realizes: the beauty it was meant to appreciate was never that the universe was physically attractive or beautiful. The beauty of the universe lies in it being temporary. The true beauty of the cosmos is not the lights or the amalgam of colors; it is what you are able to love because it is ephemeral.

The true beauty of the universe is you.
